Keep or cull

The keep/cull accounting workbook

An Excel file that shows, cow by cow and horizon by horizon, how the keep-versus-cull call is built, with the sale of the cow now as its own visible column.

The workbook is a reporting output. It does not change the model, the reward vector or the numbers of keep_cull_table; it re-presents the same solves with the immediate sale made explicit.

What it is for

Use it when you want to see why a cow is called KEEP or CULL, to enter the price a buyer actually offers for particular cows, or to give an adviser or reviewer a file in which every step is an Excel formula they can inspect. If you only need the break-even value of every cow, keep_cull_table (see the tutorial) is enough.

The terms

TermMeaning
KEEPThe expected discounted future value of the cow over the horizon, with the cost of every future replacement paid: V_total(s) − PV_rc(s).
REPLACEWhat replacing her now is worth: the sale of the cow now, plus the value of a fresh replacement heifer (state (1, 0, 0)) over the same horizon, minus the rearing cost of that heifer, charged once, now: S + V_keep(h) − C.
Estimated salvage (S_est)An indicative sale value of the cow now: body weight × dressing percentage × beef price − culling fee.
Actual net offerA price you type for a cow (column K). It is the net cash received: the culling fee is not subtracted again.
Salvage used (S)The typed offer if the cell is filled, otherwise the estimate. Never both.
ΔQKEEP − REPLACE. Positive: keeping is better. Negative: replacing is better.
Break-even offerKEEP − V_keep(h) + C: the salvage at which KEEP and REPLACE are equal. It is the value_<N>y of keep_cull_table.
DecisionCULL when the salvage used is above the break-even offer, otherwise KEEP. This is the same statement as ΔQ < 0.

Sheets and columns

The file has the sheets README, Inputs, one sheet per horizon (Cows_1y, Cows_3y, Cows_5y by default) and Checks. Grey cells are values from the model, yellow cells are inputs you may change, white cells are formulas.

The Inputs sheet holds the beef price (B2), the dressing percentage (B3), the culling fee (B4) and the annual discount (B5), then one row per horizon with the rearing cost C, V_total(h), PV_rc(h) and V_keep(h). Salvage now, salvage later, and double counting

  • Future replacement costs of the cow and of her replacements are inside PV_rc(s), so they are already paid in KEEP. The replacement cost C of the heifer bought now is charged once, in REPLACE.
  • Future cull salvage of the cow and of her replacements stays inside V_total(s): it is the model's reward for a cull at a later time.
  • Salvage now is a different event: the sale of this cow today. It is added once, on the REPLACE side only (column L into column O). KEEP does not contain it, because in KEEP she is not sold today.
  • A typed offer replaces the estimate; the two are never added together.

How it differs from the model

The MRP reward vector is milk_income + pregnancy_reward + cull_reward − mortality_cost − feed_cost − reproduction_cost. Replacement cost is not in it, and the sale of the current cow is not in it either. The model's own comparison (replacement_comparison, keep_cull_table) does not credit the sale of the current cow, which is a documented limitation of the model; it therefore reports only the break-even value. The workbook leaves that comparison and the reward vector as they are and adds the sale as a column on top.

How to generate it

pip install "cowmrp[excel]"        # openpyxl

python runners/cow/farm/keepcull.py --excel                      # default herd file, horizons 1, 3, 5 years
python runners/cow/farm/keepcull.py --excel --years 1,3 --out-dir my_run
python runners/cow/farm/keepcull.py --excel --set REARING_COST=2800   # scenario run, see below

The workbook is written as keepcull_accounting.xlsx next to keepcull.csv and summary.csv. From Python:

from cowmrp.domains.cow.keepcull_excel import write_keepcull_workbook
write_keepcull_workbook(herd, "keepcull_accounting.xlsx", model, years=(1, 3, 5))   # herd: DataFrame with a state_id column

The formulas are stored without cached results. Excel calculates them when the file is opened; a viewer that does not calculate formulas will show empty cells.

The Checks sheet

For every horizon it shows the number of cows, the sum of |break-even offer − value_<N>y of keep_cull_table|, and the number of cows called CULL with the offers entered. The sum must be zero (to rounding): it shows that the workbook's formulas reproduce the model's existing comparison. The count of CULL calls depends on the estimates and offers, not on the model. Column U of each cow sheet shows the same difference per cow.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The estimate is indicative. It uses the model's body weight of the cow's state, not her weight on the day of sale, and the model's dressing percentage, beef price and culling fee. Prices have a year and a place, listed in the economic settings table. When you have a real offer, type it.
  • A typed offer must be net: what you actually receive, after any fee or hauling.
  • Only the current cow's sale is added. Everything about the future comes from the model, with its own assumptions and limitations (see Science and Provenance).
  • The model's values (grey cells) are pasted numbers. Changing the beef price, dressing percentage or culling fee on the Inputs sheet changes the estimate only; the future cull salvage inside V_total keeps the values the model was solved with, and so do the rearing cost and the discount. To change those consistently, run a scenario with --set (it re-solves and lists the non-default settings in the workbook).
  • All values are expected values in USD over the stated horizon, for a cow in a given model state. They are not a prediction for an individual animal.
  • The comparison is for one cow against a replacement heifer. It does not model herd-level constraints such as stall space or heifer supply.
