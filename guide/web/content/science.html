Scientific background

What the model is

A short, self-contained description of the framework and of the cow model built on it. It states what the package computes; it adds no result of its own.

This page covers concepts only. The maintainers' assumption register, model audit and literature list are kept separately and will be linked from here once they are released.

Markov reward processes

A Markov reward process has three parts: a finite set of states, a transition matrix P whose entry P[i, j] is the probability of moving from state i to state j in one time step (each row sums to 1), and a reward earned in each state. With a discount factor γ per step, the expected discounted value of a state is the reward it earns now plus the discounted expected value of where it goes next. Over a finite horizon this is a sum over steps; for an infinite horizon it is the solution of a linear system.

The engine, cowmrp.mrp, implements exactly this and knows no animal. A domain supplies the states, the probabilities and the rewards; cowmrp.model.build_model assembles and checks them (every row must sum to 1, and nothing is silently rescaled).

The cow state

A cow is described by three variables, in model-step units: her lactation number, her days in milk (dim) and her days pregnant (dp; 0 when open). Health, milk yield and the like are not state variables: they enter through the probabilities and rewards that depend on the state.

Which combinations exist is decided by named rules: conception can only follow a calving, it can only happen after the voluntary waiting period, and the table has a finite range of dim and dp. At month resolution the cow model has {{CO_STATES}} states and {{CO_NNZ}} transitions. Leaving the herd (a cull or a death) sends the cow to the replacement state, a first-lactation heifer at calving, so the process stays closed and herd size is constant.

Time

The step is a month (30 days, the default), a week (7 days) or a day. dim and dp count steps, not calendar days, while fixed durations such as the length of gestation stay in real days. A state is the cow at a step boundary: a state with dim = k stands for a cow whose age in milk is uniformly spread over the step that starts at k. One setting, read once when the package is imported, is carried through states, probabilities, rewards, discounting, simulation and every exported label; set COW_TIME_UNIT before starting Python. The resolution changes the model, not only the labels: the state space, the transition probabilities (rescaled to the step), the reward of each state (the rate integrated over the days of one step) and so the expected values. Rewards

The reward earned in a state per step is

total = milk_income + pregnancy_reward + cull_reward - mortality_cost - feed_cost - reproduction_cost

Each term is its own module in cowmrp.domains.cow.rewards. Environmental quantities (enteric and manure methane, nitrous oxide, nitrogen balance, CO2) are computed from the same state and do not depend on any price; their monetary versions are separate, switched off by default, and enter the total only when enabled. The published methods, inputs, equations, assumptions and limitations of these quantities are on the Methods page.

The replacement cost is not part of the reward vector: leaving the herd is a transition, and the cost is charged where cows are compared (next section), so it is never counted twice.

Keep, replace and the break-even value

Three quantities are kept apart for a cow in a given state:

QuantityMeaning
keepthe expected discounted value of the cow, including the cost of her future replacements
replacethe value of a newly calved heifer, after the cost of one replacement
value = keep − replacethe break-even sale value: culling and replacing her now beats keeping her only if the sale brings more than this

The sale price is an input of the keep/cull table (offer); the model does not invent one. The salvage value of a sale is not credited in the model's own comparison, which is documented as a known limitation. The keep/cull workbook adds the sale of the current cow as a separate, visible column (an estimate, or an offer you type) without changing the model.

Expected, stochastic and Monte Carlo

ModeWhat it computesUse it for
expected (default)the exact distribution of the herd over states after each step; deterministic and fastplanning, scenario comparison, dashboards
stochasticone sampled path per cowone plausible herd history
monte_carlomany sampled herds: mean, standard deviation, 5th and 95th percentilewhen the spread matters, mainly small herds

For a large herd the sampled modes agree with the expected distribution on the mean and cost far more time, so nothing in the package runs them unless you select them.

Youngstock

The calf, heifer and first-calving chain is a second domain on the same engine, with {{YS_STATES}} states and {{YS_NNZ}} transitions. Its output feeds the replacement flow when the two are coupled; the example shows where a newborn calf is after 6 to 36 months.

How the package is checked

  • Every transition row is checked to sum to 1, and a missing implementation is a hard error, never a silent fallback.
  • The package's results are compared with saved outputs of the original scripts: tables are byte-identical, and value comparisons agree to within rounding.
  • The default test suite runs in about a minute; day resolution and Monte Carlo parity are separate, longer checks.
  • Passing a test shows that the code reproduces the reference model; it does not by itself show that a biological assumption is right.
